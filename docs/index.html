The big shapes, and what holds them up

Drop in a picture. You get its Notan mass (the big light and dark shapes, read the way a painter squints, so texture does not break them up), a 2, 3 or 5 value Notan, and a map of where the structure is carried by colour rather than value.

Carried by colour

Blue = mass that disappears when the colour is removed. Grey = masses that value alone holds up.

Construction

Share of the mass that stays in place when the picture is changed without moving anything. The change that destroys the most is what the structure depends on. A reading, not a verdict.

Readout

Notan

Japanese for the play of light and dark. Arthur Wesley Dow taught composition through it: reduce a picture to a few flat values and see whether the design still holds.

How the mass is read

Luminance and OKLab chroma (at half weight) are blurred at 4, 8 and 16 px and measured as distance from the picture's median. Texture disappears in the blur; a brushy shape stays one shape.

Carried by colour

The picture is turned to greyscale and read again on raw values. Wherever the mass drops, the shape existed in colour, not in value. A whole painting can score "tonal" while this map shows which parts are colour-built.

Known limits

Blurring spreads mass a little past each edge, so shapes glow at their outlines. You are measuring a photograph at one scale, not the painting.

The parallax-notan Python package, ported to JavaScript. On the package's nine test images and four paintings the port matches the Python to rounding error (mass within 1e-9, value maps and readouts identical; see tools/js_parity.py).
